## 1. Setup

The rulebook's "SET-UP SEQUENCE" (p.1):

> 1. Choose a deck
> 2. Place your Landscapes onto the table
> 3. Who goes first?
> 4. Shuffle your deck and draw 5 cards
> 5. Do you like your hand of cards?

**1.1 Choose a deck.** Two decks of 40 cards; each player takes one. (p.1)

> Accompanying this rulebook are two decks of 40 cards. In this 2-player game, each player
> takes command of one of the two decks. The game also includes eight large Landscape tiles.
> Four Landscapes accompany each deck. Four Cornfields Landscapes go with Jake's deck, while
> the four Blue Plains Landscapes are associated with Finn's deck. BMO's deck uses the Useless
> Swamp Landscapes, while Lady Rainicorn's deck uses the SandyLand Landscapes. Also included
> are a bunch of Hit Point tokens with 1's and 3's on them.

Deck minimum is 40 and you may exceed it; a maximum of 3 copies of any particular card
("Customize Your Deck", p.8):

> The minimum deck size is 40 cards, but feel free to go over that if you want to. The maximum
> number of a particular card that you can put into your deck is three. So you can't have more
> than three copies of The Pig in your deck.

**1.2 Place your Landscapes.** (p.1–2)

> With four Landscapes on your side of the board, you have four "slots" to fill with Creatures
> and Buildings. A single Lane is made up of two Landscapes that are joined together lengthwise.
>
> Once the board is set up, it creates a battlefield of 4 Lanes.

Each player has four Landscape tiles; each player's four tiles sit on their side, paired into
four Lanes. Deck customization may mix Landscape types (see §5.4).

**1.3 Who goes first.** (p.2)

> The Coolest Guy goes first. If this is your very first game, determine the first player at random.

**First Player Rule** (p.2, restated p.3):

> First Player Rule: The first player may not Floop or Fight during the very first turn of the game.

(Worked example, p.6: the first player can still play Creatures on turn 1 — Jake "played Husker
Knight" on his first turn, but "cannot Fight or Floop.")

**1.4 Shuffle and draw five.** (p.2)

> After shuffling your deck, place it face down and off to either the right or the left of your
> Landscapes. Then draw the top five cards of your deck into your hand.

**1.5 Mulligan (one-time).** (p.2)

> If you don't have at least two Creatures in your hand of five cards, you might want to mulligan.
> Taking a mulligan means you put your starting five cards back on top of your deck, reshuffle
> all 40 cards together, and then draw a new hand of five cards. Keep in mind that you only get
> to do this once at the start of each game.

## 2. Card anatomy

The rulebook labels the following parts on a Creature (diagram on Cool Dog, p.2):
**Name of Card**, **Card Type**, **Landscape Type**, **Art**, **Game Text**, **Defense (DEF)**,
**Action Cost**, **Attack (ATK)**, plus the **Exhausted/Ready** physical states.

The face of a Creature card carries (p.4, "Creatures" callout on Shark):

> This Shark has an Action cost of 2. That means it will require you to use both of your Actions
> for your turn to bring him into play. Additionally, in the upper right corner of the card you
> will find a SandyLand symbol. That means you must control at least two SandyLand Landscapes in
> order to play him. The number in the upper left is the Action Cost to play the card and also
> determines the number of Landscapes of that type you need to control to be able to play it.

So the layout is:
- **Action Cost** — number in the **upper left**; number of Actions it costs to play, and (for
  faction cards) the number of face-up Landscapes of that faction you must control.
- **Landscape Type** — symbol in the **upper right**; the faction of the card (e.g., Cornfield,
  Blue Plains, Useless Swamp, SandyLands, or the Rainbow icon).
- **ATK** — a Creature's attack value (damage it deals in a Fight).
- **DEF** — a Creature's defense value (the damage threshold at which it is destroyed).
- **Game Text** — ability text; anything written before a ">>>" is a cost to use the ability
  (p.8). A "Floop" ability is an ability in the game text box "that has the word 'Floop' next to
  it" (p.3).
- **Card Type** — Creature, Building, or Spell; a card's "Landscape Type" bar under the art
  marks Rainbow cards (p.5).

Exhausted vs Ready are physical states: Ready cards stand upright; Exhausted cards are turned
sideways. Ready → can Floop and Activate (p.3); Exhausted is the state after Flooping or
Activating-to-Fight.

## 3. Turn order

"TURN SEQUENCE" (p.2, repeated on the back cover, p.11):

> 1. Ready all of your cards
> 2. Draw a card
> 3. You have 2 Actions to spend
> 4. Do you want to Floop?
> 5. Do you want to spend 1 Action to draw a card?
> 6. Fight!
> 7. End of turn

**3.1 Ready all your cards** (p.3):

> At the start of your turn, put all of your Creatures and Buildings into the Ready position. Now
> they are able to Floop and Activate again.

**3.2 Draw a card** (p.3):

> Each player draws a card at the start of their turn. As the game goes on, your hand will shrink
> or grow. You don't refill your hand to six cards at the start of your turn.

**3.3 Receive 2 Actions** (p.3) — see §4.

**3.4 Floop** (p.3) — see §9.

**3.5 Spend 1 Action to draw 1 card** (p.3) — see §4.4.

**3.6 Fight** (p.3) — see §10.

**3.7 End of turn** (p.3):

> Your turn is over after your Fight phase.

Also (p.6): "Your turn is over as soon as all of your Ready Creatures are Activated to Fight."

**First-player restriction** (p.2–3): "the first player does not get to Floop or Fight during
the very first turn of the game." The first player's turn still includes Ready, Draw, Actions,
and the optional draw-card Action; only Floop and Fight are forbidden.

## 4. The Action economy

**4.1 Receiving Actions** (p.3):

> At the start of your turn you receive 2 Actions automatically. They don't come from your
> Landscapes or anything like that. You must spend Actions to play Creatures, Buildings, and
> Spells from your hand. Actions are generic and are used to play cards from your hand. You do
> not need to spend all of your Actions to complete your turn.

**4.2 Costs.** A card's Action Cost (upper-left number) is paid to play it; the same number is
the required count of face-up Landscapes of the card's faction (p.4, §5.3). Abilities may also
require Actions (p.8):

> Some cards have abilities that require you to pay Actions to use. The Action cost does not
> require you to control any particular type of Landscape to use it. If you use an Action on an
> ability you won't be able to spend that Action on something else. Spending Actions on Abilities
> that have an Action cost is always optional.

**4.3 Unused Actions are lost.** (p.3):

> If you don't spend all of your Actions, they will be lost and can't be saved.

**4.4 Spending Actions to draw — this IS an official rule.** The numbered turn sequence step 5
is "Do you want to spend 1 Action to draw a card?" and p.3 spells it out:

> If you don't spend all of your Actions, they will be lost and can't be saved. Why not spend your
> unused Actions to draw extra cards? You can spend one or two of your Actions to draw cards. If
> you have Actions left, you may even play a card that you just drew. There is no hand size limit
> in the game. You do not lose the game if your deck runs out of cards.

So: spending 1 Action draws 1 card (up to 2 cards/turn), a card drawn this way may then be
played with a remaining Action, there is no hand-size limit, and emptying your deck loses
nothing.

**4.5 Cost-0 cards.** (p.5):

> Creatures, Buildings, and Spells with an Action cost of 0 are always Rainbow cards. They may
> only be played during your own turn (even after spending your 2 Actions), but require no
> Landscape types to play. They may even be played if all of your Landscapes are face down.

## 5. Landscape control

**5.1 What "control" means** (p.4):

> You must first make sure you control the proper Landscape cards to play certain cards.
> Controlling something means that it's in play on your side of the board. All Creatures,
> Buildings, and Landscapes that are on your side of the board are under your control. Spells do
> not enter play, so you never control a Spell. The phrase "Your Creature" means a Creature you
> control on your side of the board.

**5.2 Controlling N face-up Landscapes of a card's faction.** The Action Cost number "also
determines the number of Landscapes of that type you need to control to be able to play it"
(p.4). Concrete examples:

- Shark (cost 2, SandyLands, p.4): "you must control at least two SandyLand Landscapes in order
  to play him."
- Palace of Bone (cost 1, Useless Swamp, p.5): "you must control at least one Useless Swamp
  Landscape in order to play it."

So a faction card with cost N requires you to control **N face-up Landscapes of that faction**.

**5.3 Cards need NOT be placed on a matching Landscape** (p.4, Creatures):

> You may place a Creature on any of your Lanes or even a Lane with a face-down Landscape. You
> do not have to put a SandyLand Creature onto a SandyLand.

For Buildings (p.5): "You do not have to put a Cornfield Building below a Cornfield. You may even
place it below a face-down Landscape." Faction identity (which Landscapes you control) is a
global requirement; placement on a particular Landscape is unrestricted.

**5.4 Face-down Landscape behavior.** Face-down Landscapes "don't provide any Landscape type
for the playing of cards" (glossary, p.9):

> So if you only have one face-up Cornfield Landscape, you would not be able to play any
> Cornfield or Rainbow cards with an Action cost of 2. A face-down Landscape still counts as a
> Lane, so you may put Creatures and Buildings there.

Creatures on face-down Landscapes are unaffected unless a card's own ability says otherwise
(p.4): "Creatures are unaffected if they sit on a face-down Landscape--unless the Creature's
ability states otherwise." A face-down Landscape is not a "Cornfield" for targeting purposes
(p.9, Target).

**5.5 Deck construction interplay** (p.8):

> You can customize your deck! You don't have to play with four matching Landscapes. You could
> even have a deck that uses a Cornfield, NiceLands, SandyLands, and Blue Plains. Just remember
> that in order to play a Creature, Spell, or Building that costs 2 Actions, you have to have 2
> Landscapes of that type in play. Note that you can still play two Cornfield Creatures with a 1
> Action cost in the same turn, even if you only have 1 Cornfield in play. If you are using four
> different types of Landscapes, all of your cards should cost 1 or less Action to play. However,
> Rainbow cards that cost 2 Actions may still be played, as they don't require a specific
> Landscape. You can use a mix of Landscapes to play Rainbow cards that cost 2 Actions.

**Rainbow cards** (p.5):

> Rainbow cards may be played using any type of Landscape or combination of Landscapes, except
> for face-down Landscapes (as they have no type).

I.e., a Rainbow card with cost N needs N face-up Landscapes of any faction(s); Rainbow cards
never care about faction. Factions of the physical game: Cornfield, Blue Plains, Useless Swamp,
SandyLands (all in the base starter set), plus Nice Lands and two more ("three other Landscape
types that don't appear in this starter set", p.8); the app wiki lists Corn Fields, Blue Plains,
Useless Swamp, Nice Lands, Sandy Lands, Rainbow, and "Icylands (Only in board Game)"
(cardwars.fandom.com, faction list) — Rainbow cards are side-faction, not a Landscape.

## 6. Playing Creatures

(p.4, "PLAYING CARDS"):

> After paying the 2 Actions, choose a Lane only on your side of the board for him to enter. You
> may place a Creature on any of your Lanes or even a Lane with a face-down Landscape. You do not
> have to put a SandyLand Creature onto a SandyLand. Once you have placed it, it stays there for
> the rest of the game, unless someone destroys it, or you play a card to move it, or you replace
> it. Creatures are unaffected if they sit on a face-down Landscape--unless the Creature's
> ability states otherwise.

**Replacement** (p.4):

> If you wish to put a Creature onto a Lane where you already control a Creature, you must remove
> or replace the Creature that is already there. You cannot replace a Creature that has been
> Flooped. It must be Ready to be replaced. Put the replaced Creature into its owner's discard
> pile and put any damage that was on it back into the pool of tokens.

Key points:
- A Creature is placed in **a Lane on your own side** only.
- You may not move a Creature into a Lane where you already control one — this is stated for
  lane changes in the glossary (p.9, "Changing Lanes"): "When changing Lanes, you may not move
  a Creature into a Lane where you already control a Creature." The only replacement path is a
  new card entering play from out of play (glossary, p.9): "The only time you can replace a
  Creature or Building is when a new Creature or Building is entering play from out of play."
- A **Ready** (never a Flooped) Creature may be replaced; the replaced Creature goes to its
  owner's discard pile and its damage tokens return to the pool.

---

## 7. Playing Buildings

(p.5):

> When you play a Building, choose one of the Lanes on your side of the board whether or not a
> Creature is there and place it below that Lane. Not under like sliding it underneath your
> Landscape! Instead, place it like in the image to the right. You do not have to put a Cornfield
> Building below a Cornfield. You may even place it below a face-down Landscape. Buildings may be
> replaced by playing another Building in the same Lane.

A Building coexists with a Creature in the same Lane (placed "below" the Lane). Buildings may be
replaced by playing another Building in the same Lane; like Creatures, a Building may not change
Lanes without a card that says so (glossary, p.9). Note: the rulebook does **not** state an
analogous "cannot replace a Flooped Building" restriction — see §13.

---

## 8. Spells

(p.5):

> The card to the left is a Spell. A Spell is held in your hand until you wish to play it. You
> must control the proper Landscapes and pay Actions to play Spells. When you play a Spell, read
> the game text to your opponent, do what it tells you to do, then discard the card. Spells are
> never placed onto a Landscape, Creature, or Building.

Spells never enter play, so you never control one (p.4: "Spells do not enter play, so you never
control a Spell."). Spells are cast from hand and discarded after resolving; a Spell's faction
and Action cost requirements apply as for other cards (§5).

## 9. Floop

**9.1 Definition** (p.3):

> Some Creatures have an ability in their game text box that has the word "Floop" next to it. To
> use a Floop ability, exhaust the card (turn it sideways) and move it to the back of the
> Landscape, next to the name of the Landscape. Some Buildings have Floop abilities. Buildings
> can stay where they are on the Landscape Card when Flooped, but they must still exhaust.
>
> Once you have properly Flooped your card, complete the action listed on the card.

**9.2 When you may Floop** (p.3):

> Note that you may Floop before spending your Actions, after spending 1 Action, or after
> spending both Actions and right before you Fight. You may Floop a Creature or Building in the
> same turn that it comes into play. You may only Floop during your own turn.

**9.3 Effects of Flooping** (p.3):

> A Flooped Creature does not Fight at the end of your turn. However, a Flooped Creature still
> fights when it is defending a Lane. If you would rather Attack with a Creature who has a Floop
> ability, just don't use the Floop ability and it will Fight instead. You are never required to
> use a Floop ability. If you don't, the Creature must Fight.

So a Flooped Creature:
- does NOT Fight on its owner's turn (it is excluded from the Fight phase);
- still defends (deals its ATK back when it is the defender);
- is never forced to Floop; if not Flooped, it must Fight (if Ready).

Flooped Creatures stay Flooped through the opponent's turn and only Ready at the start of your
own next turn (Struzan Djinn clarification, p.10 — "Creatures that Flooped during your turn
still count as Flooped Creatures during your opponent's turn").

## 10. Fight

**10.1 Requirement** (p.3 and p.4):

> All of your Ready Creatures must Fight after you have spent all of your Actions and done all
> the Flooping that you wish to do. You select the order in which your Creatures Fight, so choose
> wisely.

(p.4): "During your Fight phase all of your Creatures that are in the Ready position MUST Fight.
You get to choose the order in which your Creatures Fight."

The attacker chooses the order of its own Creatures' Fights.

**10.2 Activating to Fight** (p.3):

> When a Creature Fights, Activate it to attack by exhausting it. Place it at the top of the
> Landscape (near your opponent's Landscape) so you can tell that it is not a Flooped Creature.

(Also p.4: "When you Activate (turn sideways into the Exhausted state) a Creature to start a
Fight, look at your opponent's side of the Lane.")

**10.3 Empty opposing Lane — attack the Hero** (p.4):

> If he does not have a Creature on his side of the Lane, your Creature deals its Attack value to
> the opposing Hero's Hit Points. You can use HP tokens or paper and pencil to keep track of your
> HP.

**10.4 Creature vs Creature — simultaneous damage** (p.4):

> However, if a Creature is on the other side of the Lane when a Creature Activates to Fight, both
> will deal damage to each other. The attacker puts damage on the defender equal to the attacker's
> Attack (ATK) value. Use the 1 and 3 tokens to keep track of how much damage is on a Creature,
> counting upwards. Simultaneously, the defender will put damage on the attacker equal to the
> defender's ATK value.

**10.5 Destruction** (p.4):

> A Creature is discarded when is has damage on it equal to or greater than its DEF value.

Damage is tracked with 1 and 3 tokens counting upwards; a Creature is destroyed and discarded
(→ its owner's discard pile) when damage ≥ DEF. Buildings and Spells are not damaged by Fights
(only Creatures Fight; Buildings are not Creatures).

**10.6 Back-cover summary** (p.11):

> Each of your Creatures that did not Floop must Fight! If there is an opposing Creature in the
> Lane, they each deal damage equal to their Attack value to the other. If there is no opposing
> Creature in the Lane, your Creature deals its Attack value to your opponent's Hit Points.

---

## 11. Win condition

Intro (p.1):

> The object of the game is to reduce your opponent's Hit Points from 25 to 0. Do that and you
> are the Cool Guy!

Back cover (p.11):

> Reduce your opponent's Hit Points from 25 to 0. If you do, you win! Now you're the Cool Guy and
> get to go first the next time you play.

Each Hero starts at 25 Hit Points; reduce the opponent's HP to 0 to win. (Hit Point tokens with
1s and 3s, or paper and pencil, track HP.)

## 12. Official glossary and card clarifications (rulebook text)

### 12.1 Glossary (p.9)

**+ATK:** "When a Creature has an ATK bonus it will deal that much additional damage when it's in
a Fight."

**+DEF:** "When a Creature has a DEF bonus it can take that much more damage before it dies."

**Adjacent:** "A Creature that is in a Lane to the immediate left or right of another Creature is
adjacent to that Creature. A Creature that is in a Lane to the immediate left or right of a
certain Landscape is adjacent to that Landscape. A Building that is in a Lane to the immediate
left or right of another Lane is adjacent to that Lane (or a Creature in that Lane). Creatures,
Buildings, and Landscapes you control are only adjacent to other cards you control. Your cards
are never adjacent to your opponent's cards. In the combat examples on page 7, X-Large Spirit
Soldier is adjacent to The Pig. A Building is not adjacent to a Creature that it shares a Lane
with."

**Changing Lanes:** "A Creature or Building may change Lanes only if you have a Spell or ability
that says it can change Lanes. When changing Lanes, you may not move a Creature into a Lane where
you already control a Creature. You may not move a Building into a Lane where you already control
a Building. The only time you can replace a Creature or Building is when a new Creature or
Building is entering play from out of play."

**Destroy:** "When you destroy a Creature or Building it is placed into its owner's discard pile.
The owner of the card is the player whose deck it came from."

**Discard:** "When you are told to discard a card, that card comes from your hand. It goes to your
discard pile. You get to choose what to discard unless the effect says otherwise."

**Discard Pile:** "Cards that you discard and cards that leave play are placed into your discard
pile. The cards in your discard pile should always be face up and both players may look at them
at any time. If you have control of one of your opponent's cards and it dies, it goes to your
opponent's discard pile, not yours."

**Empty:** "An empty Lane is one that has no Creatures on your side of it. If you control three
Creatures, you control one empty Lane. A Lane with no Buildings on your side of it is empty of
Buildings."

**Face-Down Landscapes:** "Face-down Landscapes don't provide any Landscape type for the playing
of cards. So if you only have one face-up Cornfield Landscape, you would not be able to play any
Cornfield or Rainbow cards with an Action cost of 2. A face-down Landscape still counts as a
Lane, so you may put Creatures and Buildings there."

**Opponent:** "The other player in the game, not one of his Creatures or Buildings."

**Opposing:** "An opposing Creature or Building is one that your opponent controls."

**Target:** "Some Spells and abilities want you to choose a Creature, Building, or Landscape to
affect. The card you choose to affect is your target. If your card has a target, you must target
something, even your own card, unless the card says otherwise. If an ability asks you to target
something, and there are no choices that fulfill the requirements, you cannot use that ability or
Spell. For example: If The Pig has already flipped the Cornfield in his Lane face down, he can't
Floop again to flip that Cornfield face down again. A face-down Landscape is not a Cornfield, so
it cannot be a 'target Cornfield.'"

### 12.2 Other Abilities rules (p.8)

- Most abilities are static/continuous: "Most abilities are always on. These abilities last only
  last as long as that card is in play."
- Cost notation: "In a card's game text box anything written before the '>>>' is the cost to use
  the ability. You cannot use an ability if you cannot pay the cost."
- "This turn" duration: "Some cards have abilities that only last for 'this turn.' That means it
  only lasts until the end of your turn." (Woadic Chief example: "+2 ATK this turn for each Spell
  you have played this turn" — counts Spells played before or after it enters play, and the bonus
  only lasts that turn.)
- "In this Lane": "When a Creature's or Building's ability affects something 'in this Lane,' it
  means the Lane the card occupies." (The Pig can only flip a Cornfield in its own Lane, "not ...
  some other Cornfield on your opponent's side.")
- Action-cost abilities: see §4.2.

### 12.3 Specific Card Clarifications (p.10)

**Cool Dog:** "While Cool Dog lives, your opponent cannot Fight your Creatures in the Lanes
adjacent to Cool Dog's Lane. Cool Dog may still be Attacked, however. If a Creature cannot
Activate to Fight, it can still Floop. If it can't Floop, you will end your turn with these
Creatures in the Ready position."

**Cross Pollination:** "If you have four Cornfield Landscapes on your side of the playing field,
you only control one type of Landscape. The different art on the Cornfields does not mean you
control four different Landscapes. Only the name on the Landscape matters. Face-down Landscapes
have no name, so you get no bonus for them."

**Dr. Death:** "To pay the cost of using his ability, the good Doctor can destroy himself or
another Creature under your control."

**Field Reaper:** "When an opposing Creature moves to your side of the board, it does not ready
until the start of your next turn. The additional cost must be paid when you play this card. If
all of the Lanes adjacent to Field Reaper have Creatures in them, the opposing Creature in his
Lane does not move. If no Creature is in the opposing side of this Lane, the Field Reaper will
move to an adjacent empty Lane, if possible."

**Nice Ice Baby:** "This card gets the +3 ATK bonus while attacking your opponent directly. If it
Fights a Creature, it does not get the ATK bonus."

**Ms. Mummy, Sand Eyebat:** "If the target has no damage on it, it gets a permanent +1 DEF (while
in play). If the target has damage on it, remove 1 damage instead. When Ms. Mummy returns to your
hand, remove all damage tokens from her."

**Struzan Djinn:** "Creatures that Flooped during your turn still count as Flooped Creatures
during your opponent's turn. Be sure to keep your Flooped Creatures near the back of the
Landscape so you can tell the difference between Creatures who Flooped and ones who Activated to
Fight."

**Unempty Coffin:** "A Creature's cost cannot be reduced below 0."

**Rainbow-creature group clarification (Corn Lord, Cross Pollination, Woad Talisman, Ms. Mummy,
Peach Djinni, Shark, Wall of Sand, ZaZo's Magic Seeds, Gray Eyebat, Bone Wand, Snake Eye Ring):**
"Remember that Rainbow Creatures do not count as Cornfield, Useless Swamp, SandyLands, or Blue
Plains Creatures."

The combat examples (p.6–7) also encode rulings worth noting:
- **Husker Knight** ("Husker Knight has +1 ATK and +2 DEF for each Cornfield Landscape you
  control") — flipping one of the controlling player's Cornfields face down reduces its ATK/DEF
  immediately (p.6).
- **The Pig** deals damage back to the creature it is fighting, including its +1 ATK adjacency
  bonus; a Flooped Creature that did not start a Fight takes no damage even when an opponent
  attacks the Lane from the other side? — Actually the example states the reverse: "The Pig is
  Flooped, it does not Fight. It also means the Husker Knight won't deal any damage to The Pig as
  The Pig did not start a Fight this turn." (p.6). Wait — re-reading: this is the *Jake* player's
  own turn 1 example: X-Large Spirit Soldier Activates to Fight; there is no opposing Creature in
  that Lane, so it deals 1 damage to the opponent's HP. The Pig is Flooped so it doesn't Fight.
  The note that "the Husker Knight won't deal any damage to The Pig as The Pig did not start a
  Fight this turn" refers to the *opponent's* Husker Knight not being able to damage The Pig
  *during this Jake turn* — i.e., creatures only Fight when they start a Fight (their owner
  Activates them); an opposing creature cannot pre-emptively attack. The Husker Knight will deal
  damage to The Pig on the *next* (Jake-vs-Husker-Knight owner's) turn when that player Fights.
  This is consistent with §10 (only the active player's Ready Creatures Fight).
- On p.7, a Creature that dies in the Fight phase is removed; the lane becomes empty and the
  opponent's creature can attack directly next turn ("it will die during his next Fight and leave
  the Lane empty to the Husker Knight's attack on the Cornfield player's turn.").

## 13. Ambiguities and gaps (feed the engine's ambiguity-resolution policy)

Explicit gaps and unclear spots in the 2012/2013 rulebook:

1. **Two adjacent Cool Dogs.** The rulebook says adjacency is only among cards *you* control
   (p.9), and Cool Dog prevents your opponent Fighting your Creatures in Lanes adjacent to Cool
   Dog's Lane (p.10). If a player controls two Cool Dogs in the two Lanes adjacent to a given
   Lane (e.g., one Cool Dog in each outer Lane with a middle Lane between), the rulebook is
   silent on whether the protections stack or overlap in any way, and whether a Cool Dog protects
   the *other* Cool Dog (a Creature in an adjacent Lane). No stacked-effect or redundancy
   resolution is defined.

2. **Temporary vs permanent stat changes.** The rulebook defines +ATK/+DEF (p.9) and says
   "this turn" bonuses last "until the end of your turn" (p.8), while Ms. Mummy grants a
   "permanent +1 DEF (while in play)" (p.10). It does **not** define:
   - whether "until the end of your turn" extends through the Fight phase (the Fight phase is
     part of your turn, so presumably yes — e.g., Woadic Chief / Shark "+1 ATK this turn" should
     apply during that turn's Fights, and the p.6 example implies the adjacency +1 ATK applies
     during the same turn's Fight), but this is inferred, not stated;
   - whether a +DEF bonus raised *after* damage was already placed makes the Creature survive
     (i.e., is the damage-vs-DEF threshold checked dynamically), and vice-versa when a +DEF
     bonus is lost (Husker Knight example shows ATK/DEF dropping when a Cornfield flips, p.6,
     but with damage already on the creature this interaction is unstated);
   - whether an ATK bonus applies when defending (only "when it's in a Fight", p.9 — reads as
     both directions, but unstated).

3. **Simultaneous damage resolution.** Both Creatures "deal damage to each other" /
   "Simultaneously" (p.4), but the rulebook does not define the destruction-check ordering when
   *both* die (e.g., whether "leaving play"/"entering play" triggers resolve in a defined order,
   or who is "discarded" first). The engine must pick a deterministic simultaneous-death policy.

4. **Flooped Creatures still defend — exact combat timing.** A Flooped Creature "still fights
   when it is defending a Lane" (p.3), but a Flooped Creature can only be a defender if the
   *opponent* Attacks into its Lane. The rulebook gives no ordering rule for the opponent's
   Fight phase vs. "does not Fight at the end of your turn" — the simultaneous-death case with a
   defending Flooped Creature is covered by §3 but the engine should treat defender damage as
   unconditional on the defender's own Fight eligibility.

5. **First player's non-Floop Activating abilities.** The First Player Rule forbids Floop and
   Fight on turn 1 (p.2). It does not address Actions-cost abilities that exhaust a card without
   being a "Floop" (e.g., an Activate-style ability with a ">>>" cost that isn't Floop). Whether
   the first player may activate such abilities on turn 1 is unspecified.

6. **Mulligan trigger is "you might want to"** (p.2). It is phrased as a suggestion when the
   opening hand lacks 2 Creatures. The rulebook does not explicitly say whether a mulligan is
   *permitted* when the hand already contains 2+ Creatures, nor whether it is mandatory when it
   does not. Only "you only get to do this once at the start of each game" is firm.

7. **Ordering of the draw-card Action vs card plays.** Turn sequence lists "You have 2 Actions to
   spend" (step 3) before "spend 1 Action to draw a card" (step 5), yet p.3 says you "may even
   play a card that you just drew" — implying you may play a card *after* spending an Action to
   draw. The rulebook never states whether drawing with Actions may occur *before* playing cards,
   or whether playing/Floop order is otherwise locked; the Floop order is explicitly flexible
   (§9.2) but the draw action's position in the sequence is not.

8. **Replacing a Flooped Building.** Creatures may not be replaced while Flooped (p.4). The
   Buildings section (p.5) says "Buildings may be replaced by playing another Building in the
   same Lane" without the Ready restriction. Whether a Flooped Building can be replaced is
   unstated.

9. **Rainbow "type" for cost N.** Rainbow cards with cost N need N face-up Landscapes of any
   type(s) (p.5, p.8). The rulebook doesn't explicitly resolve whether a *single* face-up
   Landscape can satisfy more than one Rainbow card's requirement simultaneously for N>1 (it
   cannot, obviously, but per-card counting of the same Landscape is never formally stated).

10. **Card-face orientation of Ready vs Exhausted.** "Exhausted" = turned sideways; "Flooped"
    Creatures sit at the back of the Landscape, Activated-Fight Creatures at the top (p.3). The
    rulebook says nothing about orientation conventions for Buildings that Floop (they stay in
    place, p.3). Purely cosmetic, but a digital engine must pick a canonical representation.

11. **No "summoning sickness".** The rulebook explicitly allows Flooping "in the same turn that
    it comes into play" (p.3) and the worked example has the second player Fight with both
    Creatures the turn they are played (p.6) — so there is no summoning sickness. This is
    confirmed behavior, not a gap; noted to prevent an engine from importing one.

12. **Win by reaching 0** — the rulebook gives no tie/edge case (e.g., simultaneous-reduction to
    0 is impossible since only the active player's creatures attack; the active player may
    exceed 0-damage but HP floors at 0 — unstated). The engine should clamp HP at 0 and declare
    a single winner.
